CSS treats a declaration with var() as valid at parse time and, when the substituted value is invalid (dvh/lvh/color-mix unknown), the property becomes 'unset' instead of falling back to the earlier cascade declaration ('invalid at computed-value time'). Verified in Chromium: `.a{padding-top:50px}.a{padding-top:calc(var(--k) + 56zzz)}` computes to 0px, while the plain `56zzz` variant keeps 50px; `.c{background:red}.c{background:radial-gradient(...,nope-mix(in srgb,var(--c) 26%,transparent),...)}` computes to none.
